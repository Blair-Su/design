RESEARCH WORKSPACE · AI SIDE PROJECT

Ear to Mind: From what people say to how they think.

A research workspace for product designers to practice deeper interviews, write cognitive statements, and build mental model maps. I designed the experience around one principle: the researcher keeps the pen. Coaching helps them look again, while the interpretation stays their own.

The idea

Make space for the thinking between a quote and a conclusion.

A transcript can tell us what happened. Understanding the intention behind it takes another pass: what was the person weighing, feeling, or trying to achieve? Ear to Mind gives that work a clear sequence, from original words to reviewed statements and shared intentions.

The four lenses—Action, Thought, Feeling, and Philosophy—offer different ways to look at the same moment. The source quote stays close, so each interpretation can be checked against what the participant actually said.

The guided tour starts with a prepared dinner-planning example. Each small explanation sits beside the control it describes. People can explore, edit, skip ahead, or return later without completing an assignment first.

Tutorial 01 · Interviews

Start with what was actually said.

Paste a transcript or import interview notes, keep the speaker labels, and save a selected passage as a quote. The first tutorial introduces the evidence that everything else will return to, using a fictional conversation about deciding what to eat after a long day.

Tutorial 02 · Saved quote

Keep the source close.

The quote library holds the original passage, the participant, and a link back to the interview. This tutorial follows a saved quote into “Write a statement,” making the transition from evidence to interpretation visible while preserving the words that prompted it.

Tutorial 03 · Statement editor

Write one intention, then look again.

Choose Action, Thought, Feeling, or Philosophy and draft a cognitive statement. The editor keeps the quote beside the researcher’s wording. Coaching offers a strength, a point to reconsider, and a guiding question; the researcher revises the statement and completes six checks before placing it on the map.

The six checks

  • Starts with a verb
  • Describes a process, not a solution
  • Holds one idea
  • Fits the chosen lens
  • Stays faithful to the quote
  • Uses only the words it needs

Tutorial 04 · Intention tower

Find the intention shared across statements.

Reviewed statements can be grouped into towers using drag and drop or an accessible placement menu. Related towers sit within mental spaces. This tutorial moves from an individual statement to a pattern, with the researcher deciding what belongs together and how to name the shared intention.

Tutorial 05 · Support opportunities

Connect possibilities to a human intention.

Support opportunities sit below the line, beneath each intention tower. This tutorial introduces a place to explore how a service or product could help, while keeping those ideas separate from the research above. In the practice example, “Reduce decision effort” leads to making familiar options easier to compare.

Tutorial 06 · Practice coach

Use feedback to deepen your own interpretation.

Try a statement review and return to Coaching history as your thinking develops. Practice feedback is clearly labeled as a scripted example. In a personal project, the researcher prepares a saved review request and uses the connected Ear to Mind plugin in ChatGPT to receive coaching, then brings that perspective back to the workspace.

Reflection

Keep the evidence in view.

Connecting each statement to its quote makes interpretation something a researcher can revisit, question, and refine.

Teach at the moment of use.

A small explanation beside the relevant control gives people a next step while leaving room to explore at their own pace.

An independent learning tool informed by Indi Young’s mental model method. Tutorial and demo examples use fictional research data.
