Cube: Seeing a design problem from six angles

A brainstorming space that helps product designers move from a loose brief to a direction they can believe in. I designed and built it on my own with AI-assisted coding, from concept to a working prototype.

Product Designer & Builder

Problem
Brainstorming often jumps straight to ideas, before the problem is understood, and ends in a board full of sticky notes with no clear next step.
Solution
A four-step thinking space: frame the challenge, decompose it through six perspectives, generate ideas, and converge on a 2×2 map.

The problem

Plenty of ideas, no direction

In my own design work, brainstorming rarely failed for lack of ideas. It failed because the process had no shape. We jumped to solutions before understanding the problem, looked at it from one angle, and ended with a crowded board and no way to decide.

Whiteboard tools give you space, but not structure. I wanted a flow that was clearer to follow and simpler to use.

The idea

Turn the cube

A cube has six faces. In Cube, each face is a perspective on the same problem: User, Emotion, Business, Tech, Constraint, and Wildcard. Turning the cube means deliberately looking from an angle you'd otherwise skip.

Walkthrough

From “what if” to “let's try this”

Imagine you're redesigning a product's onboarding, and you only have a vague brief.

01 · Context

You put the question on the table: what you're solving, and who it matters to.

02 · Decompose

You turn the cube and ask a question from each angle. From the User face: What would make a first-time user feel confident enough to begin?

03 · Ideate

You answer those questions with ideas, add and arrange them on the board.

04 · 2×2 Map

You rate and place each idea on a 2×2 map, then export the board or a direction summary to take back to your team.

Building it with AI

The method first, the AI second

I built Cube with Codex, going from design to a working product on my own.

The key decision was scope. Instead of starting with AI features, I shipped the method as a manual workflow first, so I could check that the four steps hold up without AI doing the thinking. Boards save in the browser and export as files, which kept the first version simple: no accounts, no login, open and start.

What's next

Cube Companion

The next layer is an AI partner that keeps the conversation open rather than handing over answers:

  • A nudge toward the next question when a challenge feels too broad
  • A fresh pair of eyes that points out faces of the cube still empty on the board
  • A reason behind every suggestion, including why an idea sits where it does on the 2×2, so you can challenge it

Before building it, I plan to test the manual workflow with designers, to see where people get stuck and where AI would actually help.

Reflection

Structure is a design material.

The most useful thing Cube offers isn't a feature, it's the order of steps.

Build the core before the magic.

Shipping without AI made me prove the method stands on its own.
