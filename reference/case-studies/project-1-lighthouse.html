Lighthouse - Diabetes Support

Student Team Project

Year

2023

Duration

10 weeks

Overview

A diabetes travel support concept designed to make dining, insulin management, and medication routines easier on the go.

My Roles

UX Designer: I established our design system and worked with our visual design lead to balance between data-driven functionality and visual craft.

Design Outcomes

83%

Average user satisfaction with Lighthouse experience

36%

willing to download our app after completing our usability test.

8 Awards

From Indigo Design Awards (Including Gold in UX, Interface & Navigation) and European Product Design Award

📍 Context

Why is travel challenging for diabetes?

Diabetes management is continuous, but travel can make it unpredictable. We explored where support could fit more naturally into the travel experience.

Project Goal:

Create a carefree support for diabetes during travel

🔍 Research

What does diabetes management require?

Even a simple meal involves multiple decisions around food, glucose, medication, and insulin.

Before Meal:

Find Restaurant

→ Check glucose

→ Take insulin (injection/medication)

After Meal:

Monitor glucose afterward

→ Take prescribed medication / insulin if needed

Through the routine, medication management stood out:

The complex and strict medication requirements can make diabetes feel more anxious.

Emergency Kit

+

Extra Insulin · Pad & Pen; Rubbing Alcohol · Covering Cloth · Needle · Cooling pack; Continuous Glucose Monitor; Backup blood glucose meter · Test strip; Fast-acting Sugar · Snack

Learning from lived experiences

We interviewed people living with diabetes to understand how daily routines change when they travel.

🎯 Problem Reframing

What we first thought

Travel makes it harder for people with diabetes to stay on top of medication.

What we learned

The challenge wasn’t simply about medication. Travel made everyday diabetes management harder to coordinate across food, timing, privacy, and unfamiliar places.

The research findings indicate that:

01

Dining requires more planning

Finding suitable food is harder when nutritional info is unclear.

02

Injection depends on place

Users may know when to inject, but still struggle to find a safe, private space.

03

Routines become easier to miss

Travel disrupts familiar cues around medication and glucose management.

So we reframed the problem:

How might we connect the support travelers with diabetes need across food, insulin, and injection place, so that managing diabetes feels less fragmented on the go?

💡 Product Strategy

1. Fit-into existing travel behavior

Instead of building another app, we integrated diabetes support into a google map experience people already use to search, plan and navigate.

2. Support the pre-meal journey

From finding a diabetes-friendly restaurant to adding a Lighthouse stop, we make sure users can inject and prepare before their meal.

Find a DF Restaurant

Search diabetes-friendly restaurants on the map.

Add a Lighthouse Stop

Find a nearby Lighthouse and add it as a stop

Continue to Restaurant

Inject, then continue to your destination

3. Extend support after the meal

Timely reminders help users check glucose after meals for continuing support beyond navigation and into ongoing diabetes manegement.

✅ Final Solution

My Design Contribution

As a team, I have designed UI and UX of all app interface based on current Google map design system, while showing a professional and warm look and feel.

1. Medication Schedule Set Up

Lighthouse onboarding personalizes insulin reminders and navigation based on each user’s medication routine and daily habits.

2. Diabetic Friendly Restaurant & Lighthouse Navigation

Before meals, Lighthouse guides users to diabetes-friendly restaurants and nearby injection spaces, with timely reminders to support confident decision-making.

3. Blood Glucose Test Reminders & Timer

After meals, Lighthouse provides gentle blood glucose test reminders, helping users stay on track even while traveling.

✨ Reflection

The direction of the design is not achieved in one step.

We started with one single direction of design solution, but each round of research revealed a broader set of needs. Iteration helped us connect those needs into a more complete travel support experience.
